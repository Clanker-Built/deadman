A deadman's switch, built for people in danger.

You upload sensitive material. You set a schedule. If you stop checking in — because you've been detained, injured, or killed — the material is released automatically to recipients, channels, or a public page you configured in advance.

This tool is primarily for whistle-blowers, journalists with sensitive sources, dissidents, and legal counsel who need a documented controlled-release mechanism. If you're here for any other reason, read carefully before proceeding.

Read this before creating an account.

1. Release is irreversible.

Once a release fires, material is delivered and — depending on your configuration — published to the open internet. There is no recall. No "unpublish." No second chance. Mirrors and archives may preserve the release long after you change your mind.

2. You are responsible for checking in, on time, every time.

Missing a check-in — for any reason — starts the countdown. Vacation, a broken laptop, a misread timezone, a hospital stay: all valid human mistakes, all treated identically by the state machine. Set intervals you can meet even on your worst normal week. Use the grace period generously. Assume you will forget once.

3. Operational hygiene is your job, not this tool's.

  • Use Tor Browser. Do not access this site from a network that can be linked to you.
  • Strip metadata from anything you upload (EXIF, PDF author fields, document properties, filesystem timestamps). The release worker does not sanitize your material; whatever you upload is what gets released.
  • Don't reuse the email you use here anywhere else. A passkey is tied to a real device or vault — treat it as an identity.
  • Your bundle is encrypted on your device before upload. Your passkey unlocks your account. If you lose the passkey and haven't set up a recovery contact, your account is gone. The operator cannot help you recover it — and wouldn't even if they could, because that would defeat the security model.

4. You are trusting the operator of this server with a limited but real capability.

The material you upload is encrypted on your device with a key the server never sees in plaintext. Your payload cannot be read arbitrarily by the operator or their cloud providers.

However, at release time the server decrypts your bundle as part of the pipeline. The operator, if compromised, could in principle read any bundle whose release they can force. Every access of this kind is logged in a tamper-evident chain, and an external verifier watches the scheduler for unexpected activity — but if your threat model includes nation-state compromise of this specific server, do not use this service. Run your own instance.

Future versions will split the release key across multiple independent clouds so no single operator has full decrypt capability. That work is planned but not yet live.

5. This platform cannot protect you from yourself.

If you configure a policy with a 1-day interval and full-public release, and then miss a check-in because you overslept: the material goes out. The platform enforces safety cooldowns and confirmation steps, but it cannot override your deliberate decisions. If you are not sure what you want, start with private release to trusted recipients and graduate to public release only after you've lived with the tool for weeks.

6. Third-party platforms are outside our control.

If your release includes a webhook, email, or public page, we cannot guarantee the third party will accept it, keep it live, or avoid censoring it. Plan for your release to be taken down quickly. If durability matters, pre-stage copies with journalists you trust.

How it actually works

  1. Register with a passkey. No passwords. No SMS. No phone number required.
  2. Upload one or more bundles. Your files are encrypted in your browser before leaving your machine.
  3. Add destinations: named recipients (email), your own webhook, or a public landing page.
  4. Create a policy: how often you check in, how long the grace period is, which bundles release to which destinations.
  5. Arm the policy. A 24-hour activation cooldown starts; you can cancel during that window.
  6. Check in on time. One click, any time. Resets the clock on every armed policy.
  7. If you ever stop checking in: warnings go out, grace begins, eventually the release fires.

Zero-knowledge payload

Your files are encrypted with keys generated on your device. The server stores ciphertext only. Operators cannot browse your data.

Tamper-evident

Every state change is signed and hash-chained. An external verifier independently watches the scheduler. Silent failures are detectable.

Multi-cloud durability

Your encrypted bundles live on two independent object-storage providers. A primary outage does not block release; automatic consistency checks detect drift.

If this is not a fit for you:

Don't use it. A controlled-release system is a blunt instrument. For most people, sharing a password with a trusted lawyer or loved one, using a commercial legacy service, or simply publishing your material now is the right answer. This tool exists for cases where none of those work — because the material must remain secret unless you stop being able to keep it so.
